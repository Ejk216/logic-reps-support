Logic Reps Privacy Policy

Effective November 20, 2026

The short version

Logic Reps has no accounts of its own, no ads and no tracking. Before version 1.4 the app sends us nothing. From version 1.4 it can share a weekly usage summary, only if you turn that on, and when a friend you invited finishes a first board, a small record holding two random codes is saved so your app can count them. Your progress can sync through your own iCloud account, which only you can read. Apple's Game Center is optional and stays off until you turn it on. From version 1.4 the app also asks Apple's App Store, when it opens, whether a newer version is out; that sends nothing about you.

What stays on your device

Your puzzle progress, solve times, streaks, settings and lesson history are saved in a file inside the app on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. That file is never sent to us. A usage summary, if you turn it on, is a set of counts made on your device, not this file.

iCloud sync

If you are signed in to iCloud, a copy of your progress (solves and times, streak, rest days, ratings, points, quests, lessons and unfinished boards, but not your settings) is kept in the private iCloud database of your own Apple Account, so your other iPhones, iPads and Macs with Logic Reps can share it and reinstalling the app brings it back. It is stored by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. Only your Apple Account can read it: we can't see it, and nothing passes through a server of ours. Turn "Sync with iCloud" off in the app's settings to keep progress on one device.

Deleting your data

In the app, open Settings and tap Delete my data. After two confirmations it erases your progress from that device and from iCloud, and every other device that syncs with your iCloud erases its copy the next time it opens the app. It can't be undone, so the app offers to export a backup first. You can also remove the iCloud copy in the Settings app under your name, iCloud, Manage Storage (or Storage), Logic Reps; deleting the app removes the copy on that device. It also clears the first name on your invites and makes a new invite code, so links you shared before no longer point to you, and it deletes or leaves every space you share with a friend in Logic Reps. Records already saved for your old invite code stay in the public database; they are only two random codes, and your new code is not linked to them. Scores already posted to Game Center stay with Game Center, and Logic Reps Pro stays with your Apple Account. From version 1.4 it also turns usage sharing off and deletes the summaries that device shared, the next time it is online and signed in to the same iCloud account.

Game Center

Comparing with friends uses Apple's Game Center, and only if you turn it on inside the app. While it is on, the app sends four kinds of score to Game Center: your times on the day's puzzles when you solve them (with the number of hints a time took, if any), your longest day streak, how many puzzles you have solved, and your points for the week. It also reports the achievements you have earned. Game Center shows them under your Game Center name to your friends and, depending on your Game Center settings, to other players. The app also shows you your Game Center friends' scores.

If you allow it when Apple asks, the app reads your Game Center friends list to show which of your Game Center friends play Logic Reps and whether they have a time today, in the Friends tab. It stays on your device and isn't kept.

With Game Center on, the Friends screen can also show Everyone: the day's quickest times worldwide, read from Game Center's own leaderboards, with other players' Game Center names. The Friends widget keeps your friends' Game Center names and weekly points on your device, in the app's own storage, so the widget can show them; they go nowhere else.

Game Center is run by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. The scores go from your device to Apple; we run no server and receive nothing. Turn Game Center off in the app's settings to stop posting. Scores already posted stay with Game Center.

AI coaching

The coach uses Apple Intelligence, which runs on your device. The puzzle positions and questions you give the coach are processed on your iPhone and are not sent to us. On devices without Apple Intelligence the coach is simply turned off.

Purchases

Logic Reps Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment; we never see your name, payment details or Apple Account. The app only learns from the App Store, on your device, whether Pro is unlocked.

Reminders and backups

The optional daily reminder and streak reminder are scheduled on your device from what is already stored there; no server is involved. If you export a backup, it is a file you choose where to save, and it goes nowhere else.

Sharing

If you tap Share on a daily result, a streak or a challenge, or Invite a friend, the text you choose to share goes only to the app or person you pick in the iOS share sheet. An invite link holds a random code made on your device, so the app can tell when a friend you invited plays with you, and the first name you typed if you chose to add one. A challenge link holds the game, the level, a board number and your time. While you're new, a time you send back to the friend who invited you also holds your code, theirs and the first name you typed (if any). The codes are random, aren't tied to your Apple Account or Game Center, and go only to the people you send the link to. A first name can't contain a link, a number or a rude word.

Sending an invite with AirDrop, including by holding two iPhones together, is Apple's AirDrop: the app hands the link to the iOS share sheet and doesn't see who receives it.

The Logic Reps App Clip, which opens a shared board for someone who doesn't have the app, keeps everything on the device and sends nothing. It has no usage sharing.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages. It never asks for a name, an email or an age. Usage sharing (from version 1.4) is off until someone turns it on.

Racing in Messages

The Logic Reps app in Messages (iPhone and iPad) sends a race as an ordinary iMessage message to the people in that chat: the board's number, and each player's time, mistakes and hints. Players are told apart by the random IDs Messages gives each person in a chat; the app never reads names or phone numbers. For your first 60 days after joining through a friend's invite, your race results also carry your random code, the code of the friend who invited you and the first name you typed (if any); everyone in that chat's race receives them, so their apps can tell you played together. So that the race board looks like yours, the Messages app reads a copy of your settings, board theme and today's results that the Logic Reps app keeps in its own storage on your device. A race you finish is passed back to the app on the same device. Nothing goes to us.

Usage summaries (from version 1.4)

Usage sharing is off until you turn it on. The app asks once, on the result card after a solve, when you have played on at least three days and the device is signed in to iCloud. The card has two buttons, Share and Not now. Not now is final: the app doesn't ask again. The switch is in the app's settings under Privacy, "Share usage with the developer", and you can turn it on or off there at any time. Nothing is counted or sent while it is off, and nothing from before you turned it on is included.

While sharing is on, the app keeps counts on that device and, about once a week, saves a summary of the week that has ended. A summary holds: the week it covers; how many days of that week you played; the app's version; whether the device is an iPhone, an iPad or a Mac; minutes in the app, in each game, and on the Collection, Friends, Progress, Lessons and Trainer screens; for each game and level, how many boards you started, finished and left unfinished, and how many hints you took; hints by technique; first walkthroughs finished or skipped, by game; whether Ease me in is on; if adding a friend or showing a friend's times through iCloud failed, the name of the error iCloud gave and the step it failed at, and how many times; and how often you opened a lesson you hadn't done, finished a round of Race a friend, showed the QR code, or finished all of today's six. Apart from the days played, the minutes and counts are rounded down into ranges, such as "3 to 5" or "15 to 29 minutes". A summary never holds your name, your email, a board number, a solve time, a time of day or your progress file. The App Clip and the Logic Reps app in Messages count nothing and send nothing.

A summary is saved from your device to the public database of Logic Reps' own iCloud container, which Apple runs under Apple's privacy policy. Each summary is named with a random ID made on your device and the week, so the same week saved twice counts once. The app replaces that random ID every 90 days and whenever sharing is turned off and on again. Apple stamps each summary with the time it was saved and an ID for your iCloud account in this app. We can see that ID. It is not your name or email, and we don't use it to work out who you are. It stays the same when the random ID changes. We read the summaries to see which games and screens get used and where boards are left unfinished, so we can make the app better. Apple stores the summaries; no other company receives them, and they are never used for ads.

To stop, turn the switch off or use Delete my data. Either one erases the counts on that device and deletes the summaries that device sent. If the device is offline or signed out of iCloud, the delete waits and runs the next time the app is open, online and signed in to the same iCloud account. If the device is later signed in to a different iCloud account, sharing turns itself off there and stays off until someone turns it on again. iCloud lets only the account that saved a summary delete it, so the app says in its settings when a delete was turned away, and tries again each time it opens; it goes through once the device is signed in to the first account again. Summaries more than 12 weeks old are removed the next time the device sends one. Each device deletes only what it sent, so turn sharing off on every device where you turned it on, and do it before deleting the app: deleting the app alone leaves the summaries it already sent. Questions about your summaries: contact@arctixcapital.com.

Friends in Logic Reps (from version 1.4)

When you add a friend with a link, a QR code or AirDrop, Apple's iCloud sets up a private space for the two of you. Until someone accepts your invite, anyone holding that link can join it, so send it only to the person you mean; once the first person has joined, the app closes the link and removes anyone else who joined. An invite nobody accepts is deleted after a week. Each of your apps writes your name for friends (if you typed one), your times on today's puzzles, whether you used a hint, and your day streak there. It's stored in iCloud, not on our servers, and we can't read it. Remove a friend at any time: if you made the space it's deleted, and if they did you leave it and your day is removed from it. Delete my data does the same for every friend. Adding friends needs an iCloud account; it doesn't use Game Center. If you scan a friend's code with Scan a friend's code (from version 1.4), the app uses the camera only to read the code: the picture stays on your device and is never saved or sent.

Invites that count (from version 1.4)

When you share your invite link or QR code, it holds a random code made on your device and, only if you typed one, your first name. Nothing is counted when you share it. When a friend opens your link and finishes a first board in the app, their device saves one small record to the public database of Logic Reps' own iCloud container. The record holds two random codes, yours and the one their device made, and nothing else: no name, no Apple Account, no Game Center ID, no board and no time. Your device reads the records that carry your codes and counts each friend once, so it can give you free items. Apple adds the time a record was saved and an ID for the iCloud account that saved it in this app, and we can see those, so this is not anonymous to us. There is no server of ours and no third-party SDK, and nothing is shared with anyone else. A record is not deleted when you delete the app. Telling the invite count needs an iCloud account; without one, the friend still plays and you are not told.

Pausing a feature (from version 1.4)

So that we can pause friends in Logic Reps, usage summaries or the invite count without an update if one of them misbehaves, the app reads one setting from the public database of Logic Reps' own iCloud container when it opens, at most every few hours. It only reads; it sends nothing about you. A paused feature is hidden and nothing on your device is deleted.

Checking for a new version (from version 1.4)

When the app opens, and when you come back to it after several hours, it asks Apple's public App Store lookup (itunes.apple.com) which version of Logic Reps is current. The request names the app and the country set in your device's region settings, and nothing else: no name, no account, no device or install ID, no progress and no cookies. Apple receives it the way any website receives a request, including your IP address, under Apple's privacy policy. We don't run that server, don't receive the request and keep nothing from it. If a newer version is out, the Play tab offers to open the App Store page, with Update and Not now; Not now is remembered on your device so the offer doesn't come back for a week, unless a newer version appears. Offline, the app asks nothing and says nothing. The App Clip and Messages don't do this, and the Mac app leaves updates to the Mac App Store.
